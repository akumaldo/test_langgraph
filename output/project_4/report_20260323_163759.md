# Analysis of Factors Affecting Final Grade (G3)

**Summary:** The analysis identifies prior academic performance (G1 and G2) as the dominant factors influencing final grades, with correlations exceeding 0.80. Parental education levels and the intention to pursue higher education also show significant positive impacts, while negative correlations are observed for age, absences, and romantic relationships. Behavioral metrics such as study time and family relationships have a comparatively weaker influence on the final outcome.

## Step 1: Correlation Matrix Analysis

The correlation matrix reveals that previous grades are the strongest predictors of the final grade (G3). G2 shows the highest positive correlation (0.90), followed closely by G1 (0.80). The strongest negative correlation is found with the number of failures (-0.36), while age (-0.16) and time spent going out (-0.13) also show negative relationships with final performance.

## Step 2: Categorical Variable Analysis

Analysis of categorical variables highlights significant disparities based on educational and demographic factors. Students attending the GP school and those with a 'yes' status for pursuing higher education achieved notably higher mean G3 scores (10.49 and 10.61 respectively) compared to their counterparts. Additionally, students with a father as guardian and those in smaller family sizes (LE3) demonstrated higher average grades than other groups.

## Step 3: Top Numeric Predictors

The top five numeric predictors were confirmed as G2, G1, Medu (mother's education), Fedu (father's education), and studytime. Statistical summaries indicate that G2 and G1 have similar distributions with means around 10.7 and 10.9, while parental education levels (Medu/Fedu) are concentrated between 2 and 4. Study time shows a lower mean (2.04) but remains a key variable in the predictive model.

## Step 4: Visual Analysis

Scatter plots were generated to visualize the relationships between the top numeric predictors and G3. These visualizations (saved as g3_predictors.png) confirm the strong linear relationships observed in the correlation matrix for G1 and G2, while illustrating the weaker trends for parental education and study time.
